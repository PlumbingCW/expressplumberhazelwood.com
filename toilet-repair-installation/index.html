Emergency Toilet Repair in Hazelwood, MO.

A toilet that is overflowing or leaking at the base is an emergency, because the water is already headed for your floor. Our 24/7 line is staffed, and a licensed plumber can walk you through stopping the water at the toilet before we schedule the visit.

When a toilet is an emergency.

A toilet still rising toward the rim can be stopped in about ten seconds. Lift the tank lid, press the rubber flapper down over the opening at the bottom, then turn the stop valve on the wall behind the bowl clockwise until it stops. Do not flush again.

Water that keeps coming, or sewage showing up in a tub or floor drain, means the clog sits further down the line than the toilet. Shut off the main water valve if the floor is wet and call us any hour. We offer same-day appointments when available, and we can tell you over the phone which fixtures to leave unused.

  • Bowl rising toward the rim and not draining.
  • Water spreading from the base of the toilet.
  • Supply line or tank spraying or dripping onto the floor.
  • Gurgling or sewage in a nearby tub, shower or floor drain.
  • The only bathroom in the house is out of service.

Why a toilet keeps running.

A running toilet wastes water all day and rarely makes enough noise to warn you. Most of the time the flapper is not sealing, the chain has the wrong slack, or the fill valve is not shutting off at the right level. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

Clogs, plungers and knowing when to stop.

Use a flange plunger, the kind with a rubber sleeve that folds out of the cup, because it seals the toilet outlet better than a flat sink plunger. Push gently first to clear the air, then pump with steady strokes for about 20 seconds. If the water drops, flush once and watch the bowl before you trust it.

A toilet auger gets past the bend in the trap without scratching the porcelain, which a metal coat hanger will. Toys, wipes and thick paper are the usual finds.

If the plunger and auger fail, or the same toilet clogs again within a day or two, the blockage is probably past the toilet. That is a job for drain cleaning, and we can run a camera if the clog keeps coming back.

Wax rings, rocking toilets and leaks at the base.

A wax ring is a donut of wax between the toilet outlet and the floor flange. It seals water and sewer gas in. When it fails you see water at the base after a flush, smell sewer gas, or feel a soft spot in the floor.

  1. Shut off and empty. We close the stop valve, flush the tank, and sponge out the bowl.
  2. Lift and inspect. The flange, bolts and the floor around them are checked for cracks and rot before anything goes back.
  3. Reset on a new seal. A new wax ring or wax-free seal goes down, and the toilet is set level and tightened evenly.
  4. Test for leaks. We flush several times and watch the base, the supply line and the tank bolts.

If the subfloor around the flange is soft, a new ring will not hold. We tell you that before the toilet goes back, because the floor repair has to come first.

Repair it or replace it.

Replacement makes sense when the porcelain is cracked, the toilet clogs often even on a clean drain, or repairs keep stacking up. A crack in the tank or bowl leaks and cannot be sealed. Older toilets that use 3.5 gallons or more per flush are also worth swapping, since WaterSense labeled models use 1.28 gallons or less.

An install covers removing the old toilet, checking the flange, setting the new one on a fresh seal, fitting a new supply line and testing it. Moving a toilet to a different spot in the room means new drain and vent piping, which we handle as plumbing installation. We do not patch cracked porcelain.

Local note

Hazelwood water and the rubber parts in your tank.

Missouri American Water, which supplies Hazelwood, uses chloramines as its disinfectant, according to its 2023 annual water quality report. Chloramines are hard on rubber parts such as flappers and fill valve seals, which can soften, warp or turn slimy. If your flapper feels sticky or the toilet starts running again soon after a repair, that wear is the usual reason.

When we replace a flapper we suggest a chloramine-resistant one, and we suggest you look at it every year or two. The same report put hardness at the Missouri River plants at roughly 5 to 13 grains per gallon in 2023, so mineral crust on the rim jets and fill valve is common. Test your own tap, or call Missouri American Water at 866-430-0820, to see what your street gets.

Toilets sold in the United States are limited to 1.6 gallons per flush by federal law, and EPA WaterSense labels models at 1.28 gallons or less. Bowls are made to ASME A112.19.2, which is the standard that covers vitreous china fixtures.
