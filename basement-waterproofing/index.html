Basement Waterproofing in Hazelwood, MO.

A damp basement, a musty smell or a puddle that returns after every storm all point to the same problem: water is getting under or through the walls and floor. We handle the inside side of that problem with drain tile and a sump pit, and we tell you plainly when the fix is outside our scope. Urgent flooding can still call the 24/7 line.

How interior waterproofing works.

Water does not need a big hole to get in. When soil around a foundation stays saturated, pressure pushes water up through the joint where the floor meets the wall and through tiny pores in block and concrete.

An interior system accepts that water will arrive and gives it a controlled path. A channel runs along the inside of the footing, collects the water under the slab edge and carries it to a sump pit, where a pump sends it outside.

  1. Cut the channel. We open a strip of slab along the wall where the floor meets the foundation.
  2. Lay the drain tile. Perforated pipe goes in the trench, surrounded by clean stone so water moves in and silt stays out.
  3. Set the sump pit. The pipe drains to a basin with a pump, a check valve and a discharge line.
  4. Patch the floor. We restore the slab over the channel so the finished floor is flat again.

Where the water comes from.

Before any system, we look at where water enters. Wall seepage after a rain, a damp line at the base, and a wet floor along the edges are all signs of groundwater. A damp patch under a single window, or wetness that starts at a downspout, may be a drainage fault outside.

What we do and what we do not do.

We install interior drain tile, sump pits and pumps, and the discharge line that carries the water out. We do not excavate outside the house, and we do not do structural or foundation repair.

If a wall is bowing, a crack is wide or shifting, or the floor has heaved, a structural specialist should evaluate the foundation before anyone seals or drains anything. An inside system manages water. It does not fix a moving wall.

We also do not promise a bone-dry basement. A system that handles groundwater cannot cure humidity or condensation from the air, and a dehumidifier may still be needed.

Basement waterproofing is quoted per job, because every foundation is different. We do not post dollar figures for it, since the price swings with the layout of the basement. We give a clear price before work starts, after seeing the space.

Local note

Saturated ground, frost and permits in Hazelwood.

Hazelwood sits close to creeks that drain to the Missouri River, and ground around a house stays wet for days after heavy rain. If your lot is low or slopes toward the foundation, expect water pressure to build during wet weeks.

The City of Hazelwood publishes a frost depth of 30 inches. Frozen soil can seal the ground around the foundation in winter and push thaw water toward the walls in late winter, so the first warm spell is a good time to watch for a wet floor. Plumbing permits in Hazelwood run through St. Louis County, and we handle that paperwork when a system needs one.

The International Residential Code (IRC), which the City of Hazelwood lists in its 2021 edition, covers foundation drainage in section R405 and foundation waterproofing and dampproofing in section R406. Those sections set the standard for how water is meant to be kept away from a basement wall, and an interior system is a retrofit for homes that did not get it right.

Fix the outside first.

Many wet basements get better with cheap outdoor changes. Clean the gutters, extend each downspout several feet from the wall, and keep soil sloped away from the house.

If the floor still seeps after that, an inside system is the next step. The sump pit at the end of it is covered on our sump pump installation and repair page. If a wet floor shows up with no storm at all, a hidden supply or drain leak is a better suspect, and leak detection can rule it in or out.
