Finding a Leak in the Main Water Line to Your House.

The main water line is the pipe that brings water from the street to your house. A leak in it often hides underground, so the signs are indirect. Look for a higher water bill, a wet or sunken patch in the yard, low pressure, or a water meter that keeps moving when every fixture is off.

A meter test is the fastest way to confirm a leak. This guide shows how to run one, how to tell whether the leak is on the utility side or your side, and how a plumber finds the exact spot. Missouri American Water can be reached at 866-430-0820 on weekdays from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.

Signs the main line is leaking.

Underground leaks rarely announce themselves. These are the signs homeowners notice first.

  • A water bill that rises with no change in how you use water.
  • Soggy or unusually green grass in a strip between the meter and house.
  • A damp spot in the yard, driveway or basement wall where the line enters.
  • Low water pressure throughout the house.
  • The sound of running water when everything is off.
  • A meter dial or leak indicator that moves with fixtures closed.

One sign alone does not prove a leak. Together, especially a moving meter and a wet yard, they point to the main line.

Run a meter test.

  1. Turn everything off. Close all faucets, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure no one flushes. Turn off the ice maker.
  2. Find the meter. It is usually in a ground box near the curb or street. Lift the lid carefully and watch for insects.
  3. Read the leak indicator. Many meters have a small triangle or wheel that spins when any water flows. If it moves with all fixtures off, water is going somewhere.
  4. Note the reading and wait. Write the numbers, wait 30 minutes to an hour without using water, and read again. A change confirms a leak.
  5. Close the house valve and retest. If the meter stops once the main valve at the house is shut, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house.

Water service lines are commonly polyethylene pipe made to the AWWA C901 standard, and drinking water piping should be certified to NSF/ANSI 61. Knowing the pipe material helps a plumber choose the right locating method and repair.

How a plumber pinpoints the leak.

Once the meter test shows a leak on your side, the next job is to find the spot without digging up the whole yard. A plumber uses tools that listen to the pipe or track the water. Each has strengths, and each has limits.

Acoustic listening picks up the hiss of water escaping under pressure. It works well on metal pipe and in quiet conditions, and less well on plastic pipe or on a noisy street. Pressure testing isolates sections of the line to show which one loses pressure. Thermal scanning can help where a hot line runs, but it rarely helps on a deep cold supply line.

We cannot promise a single tool will find every leak. In some cases the plumber narrows the area, then opens the ground at the most likely spot. Our leak detection service combines the tools to keep digging to a minimum.

A leak you ignore does not stay small. The water bill you pay while it runs is one cost, and soil washout near the foundation is another.

When to call a plumber.

Call when the meter moves with the house valve closed, when you see water bubbling in the yard, or when pressure drops with no clear cause. Shut the main valve at the house if water is pooling. If the water is coming from the meter box itself, call the utility.

A line that has leaked in one spot often has other weak sections, especially on older pipe. A water line repair or replacement fixes the section or the whole run. When the leak is inside the walls, burst pipe repair covers the emergency fix, and repiping handles a house whose plumbing keeps failing. We serve homes in Florissant and Bridgeton too.

For a leak that is flooding the property, use the 24/7 line. We offer same-day appointments when available.
