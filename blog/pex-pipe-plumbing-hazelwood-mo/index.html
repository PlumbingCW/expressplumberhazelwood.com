Plumbing With PEX Pipe: What Hazelwood Homeowners Should Know.

PEX is a flexible plastic pipe used for hot and cold water supply inside homes. It bends around corners, comes in long coils and joins with crimp or expansion fittings, which makes it faster to install than copper in most remodels. It is not used for drains, for gas, or for outdoor runs exposed to sunlight.

For a homeowner, the question is not whether PEX is good. It is where to use it, how to protect it, and what to ask your plumber about fittings, manifolds and permits before the walls close.

What PEX is and where it fits.

PEX stands for cross-linked polyethylene. It carries pressurized water, and it is color coded, with red for hot, blue for cold and white for either. Common sizes are 3/8, 1/2, 3/4 and 1 inch.

Plumbers use it for new bathrooms, kitchens, basements and whole-house supply replacements. It is not approved for drains, and most codes limit it indoors because ultraviolet light weakens it within weeks of direct sun exposure.

Home run, trunk and branch, and manifolds.

Two layouts are common. A trunk and branch system uses a main line with branches to each fixture, similar to copper. A home run system runs a separate line from a central manifold to each fixture.

A manifold lets you shut off any single fixture without losing water to the whole house. It also helps with pressure when two fixtures run at once, since each has its own line. The trade-off is more pipe and a good location for the manifold, usually near the water heater.

  • Trunk and branch uses less pipe and fewer holes.
  • Home run gives per-fixture shutoffs and steadier pressure.
  • Mixed layouts are common in older homes where walls limit access.

Cold weather in Hazelwood and what it means for PEX.

The City of Hazelwood publishes a frost depth of 30 inches and a winter design temperature of 2 degrees F. For PEX, that matters in unheated spaces such as crawl spaces, garages and exterior walls. PEX tolerates freezing better than copper, but it can still split at a fitting, so insulation and heat matter.

In January 2024, Missouri American Water crews responded to 16 main breaks in one day, and one local plumber reported more than 180 calls. Pipes burst as things thaw, so know where your shutoff is. A PEX manifold with labeled valves makes that faster, since you can isolate one branch and keep the rest running.

PEX tubing is made to ASTM F876, PEX systems are covered by ASTM F877, and drinking water pipe should be certified to NSF/ANSI 61. Look for those markings printed on the tubing itself before it goes into a wall.

Fittings, tools and the details that matter.

PEX is easy to cut but not forgiving about joints. Crimp rings, clamp rings and cold-expansion fittings each need the matching tool and the matching pipe type. Mixing parts from different systems can leave a slow leak that shows up weeks later.

Brass fittings can be a restriction point because they reduce the inside diameter of the pipe. Expansion fittings, which stretch the pipe, keep a larger bore and flow better, which is useful for long runs or a large tub. Ask your plumber which system they use and why.

  1. Plan the layout. Mark fixtures, the manifold and the main shutoff before cutting pipe.
  2. Cut square and clean. A crooked cut or a nick in the pipe makes a weak joint.
  3. Make each joint with the right tool. Use the ring and tool made for that system.
  4. Support the pipe. Fasten it at the spacing in the manufacturer's instructions so it does not sag or rattle.
  5. Pressure test. Bring the system up to pressure and watch it before covering.

Rules for using PEX in a Hazelwood home.

PEX must be installed following the manufacturer's instructions and the plumbing code in force. Do not run PEX within a set distance of a flue, recessed light or gas water heater vent. Many installers use metal pipe for the first stretch off a water heater.

Hazelwood contracts with St. Louis County for plumbing permits and inspections, and plumbing work is done under a county license. Ask your plumber to confirm permit steps for a repipe. Florissant's code shows owners may pull a homeowner permit for their own work but not for slab-enclosed piping, so verify your own rules.

When to call a plumber.

A new PEX branch to a single fixture is within reach of a handy owner, but a whole-house job or anything that ties into the main is not. Leaks inside walls from a bad fitting are expensive to chase.

Call if you see unexplained water use, low pressure after a repipe, or a spot of moisture near a fitting. Our leak detection page explains how hidden leaks are found.
