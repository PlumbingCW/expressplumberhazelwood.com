Hydro Jetting a Main Sewer Line: Steps, Limits and Cost.

Hydro jetting clears a main sewer line by sending high-pressure water through a nozzle that scours the pipe wall. Unlike a cable, which punches a hole in a clog, a jetter washes grease, scale, silt and root hair off the whole circumference of the pipe. It is the right choice for heavy buildup. It is the wrong choice for a collapsed or badly cracked line.

This guide explains the process, when jetting beats snaking, what to check first and what national cost guides show. A camera look before jetting is a smart step on any line that has clogged before.

Cable or jetter: which one clears a main line.

A cable cuts a path through a clog, and it is quick and cheap. The trouble is that a cable leaves grease and scale on the pipe wall, so the line can clog again in weeks. A jetter flushes that material down the line and leaves the pipe closer to its full diameter.

How a main line jetting visit works.

  1. Locate the cleanout. The access point for the main line is usually a capped pipe near the foundation, in the basement or in the yard.
  2. Run a camera first when needed. A camera shows whether the line has a break, a belly or a collapse. Jetting a broken pipe can wash out soil and make the damage worse.
  3. Feed the nozzle in. The hose carries a nozzle with rear-facing jets that pull it forward and spray the wall.
  4. Work back and forth. The plumber pushes the nozzle past the clog, then pulls back slowly, scrubbing as it goes.
  5. Flush and confirm. Water is run through the house and a second camera pass can confirm that the pipe is clean.

Plan for some mess. Water and loosened debris flush through the line, so the plumber will ask you to keep fixtures off during the work and may recommend running a camera afterward. If your home has a floor drain near the cleanout, clear the area around it first.

Roots are a special case. A jetter can cut small roots, but a thick root mass often needs a cutting head first. If roots keep coming back, that points to a gap in the pipe and a repair.

What national guides say about price.

National cost guides put hydro jetting a main line at about $250 to $800, though other sources run to $1,400, so roughly $250 to $1,400 across sources. Snaking a main usually runs $150 to $500. A camera inspection is $125 to $500. A severe main with jetting is listed as $300 to $800 or more.

These are national ranges, not local prices. We give a clear price before work starts.

Local note

Hazelwood sewer ownership and digging notes.

The Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District maintains the public sewers in this area, and the private lateral from your house to the public main is generally the owner's responsibility. Neighboring Kirkwood says the same on its site, and MSD at 314-768-6260 can confirm your own case. That decides who pays when a jetting visit finds a problem past the property line.

If jetting and a camera reveal damage, the repair may involve digging. Call 811 first, as the City of Hazelwood asks, since gas, electric and water lines often run near sewer laterals. Plumbing permits in Hazelwood are handled by St. Louis County through the city.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) sets minimum drain slope at a quarter inch per foot for pipe 2.5 inches or smaller and an eighth inch per foot for 3 to 6 inch pipe. A line that has settled below that slope will hold water and clog again, however well it is jetted.

What jetting cannot fix.

  • A collapsed or crushed section of pipe.
  • A large offset joint where two sections have shifted.
  • A belly, or low spot, that holds water no matter how clean the pipe is.
  • Pipe so brittle that pressure could break it, which a camera check should flag.

For those, the answer is repair or replacement. Jetting just gives you a clean pipe to inspect. We do not promise it will end clogs on a line with structural damage.

When to call a plumber.

Call when water backs up in the tub, shower or basement floor drain, when toilets gurgle, or when a cable clears the line and it clogs again in a month or two. Homeowner jetting kits that attach to a garden hose have too little pressure to scour a main line, so they will not match a professional unit.

Our hydro jetting service handles heavy buildup, and sewer camera inspection shows what is in the line before any pressure goes in. If the lateral needs a full clean, sewer line cleaning covers it. When a camera finds damage, sewer line repair is the next step. We work in Florissant and Maryland Heights as well as Hazelwood.
