Repiping in Hazelwood, MO.

Repiping means replacing the pipes that carry water to your fixtures, and it is a big decision for a house that has been patched more than once. We help you work out whether new pipe is worth it, or whether a repair will do.

Signs that point to repiping.

One leak is a repair. A pattern of leaks is a different story. When a pipe fails in one spot and then another a few months later, the rest of the pipe is usually the same age and in the same condition.

  • Repeated pinhole leaks, even after repairs.
  • Brown or rust-colored water when you open a tap.
  • Low pressure at several fixtures, not just one.
  • Green or white crust on pipe, or visibly corroded joints.
  • Galvanized steel pipe, which rusts from the inside.

Pressure loss deserves a closer look before anyone cuts a wall. Mineral buildup inside old galvanized pipe narrows it over time, but a failing pressure regulator or a partly closed valve can cause the same symptom. We check those first.

PEX or copper.

Both materials work well when they are installed correctly, and the better choice depends on the house and what you value. We use the one that suits the job, and we explain the tradeoffs rather than push one.

Neither pipe is a cure for a bad pressure regulator or a failing water heater, and both need correct support and clearances. Ask which pipe each plumber proposes and why.

Whole house or partial.

When a partial repipe is enough.

If the problem sits in one run, such as the kitchen supply or the line to a bathroom, replacing that section can fix it. This is also a good fit when part of the house was already updated and the rest is still sound.

When the whole house makes sense.

If pipes of the same age are failing in many places, patching one run just moves the leak. A whole house repipe replaces the supply lines from the main valve to every fixture in a single project. It is more disruptive up front, and it ends the cycle of repairs.

What a repipe involves.

A repipe is planned in phases so you are not left without water for long. We walk the house, find the main shutoff and decide where the new lines will run, then open small access holes in walls and ceilings where we need them.

  1. Plan the routes. We decide where each new line goes and where access is needed.
  2. Shut off and drain. Water is off for part of a day or longer, and we tell you in advance.
  3. Run the new pipe. New lines go in with correct support and connections, then are pressure tested.
  4. Close the access and test again. We restore flow, check each fixture and leave the access ready for a drywall patch.

Patching and painting the openings is a separate trade, and we do not do it. Plan for that before the project starts.

What drives the price of a repipe.

Repiping cost depends on the size of the house and how reachable the pipes are, so we do not post a number without seeing it. We give a clear price before work starts, after a walk-through.

Permits and licensing in Hazelwood.

Plumbing work in Hazelwood requires a plumber licensed through St. Louis County, and the city contracts with the county for plumbing permits and inspections. A repipe generally needs a permit and an inspection, which protects you if the house is sold later.

Check the license of anyone you hire before they start. If you are weighing a patch against a full replacement, our burst pipe repair page explains when a repair holds. If the leak is in the underground line to the street, see water line repair and replacement. We also work in Maryland Heights and the surrounding towns.

PEX tubing systems are covered by ASTM F877, and fittings and pipe that carry drinking water should be certified to NSF/ANSI 61 and the lead-free requirements of NSF/ANSI 372. A repipe quote should name the pipe material and its standard.

Honest limits.

Repiping does not fix a drain problem, a bad water heater or low pressure from the utility. We do not guess at the cause. We check pressure at the house and rule out simpler fixes first, and if the pipes are fine we say so.

We also do not repipe a house on the phone. A walk-through is how we find out what the walls and the pipe run really look like.
