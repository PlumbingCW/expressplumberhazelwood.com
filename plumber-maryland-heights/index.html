Three questions we ask on every Maryland Heights call.

A town with 13,425 housing units in 2020 and big commercial buildings gives us a range of calls. Before we send anyone, we ask three things.

First, is it a house, an apartment or a commercial space. Second, who owns the plumbing, you, a landlord or a property manager. Third, can you reach a shutoff, and is anyone else in the building affected.

Those answers decide whether we send a plumber with a drain machine, a water heater, or a pipe fitting kit, and whether a manager has to approve the work.

Local note

Big buildings in town and what they mean for a plumbing call.

Westport Plaza covers more than 700,000 square feet at Page Avenue and I-270. In a large complex, a leak in one space can drain into another, so we ask for the building engineer before we ask for a ceiling tile.

Hollywood Casino opened in 1997, Aquaport in 1999 and the Centene Community Ice Center in 2019. Venues with heavy public restroom traffic put steady load on drains, and repeat clogs there often point to fixture volume instead of a failing pipe. We cover that kind of work under commercial plumbing. We ask each caller about the specific building instead of assuming how it is piped.

Larger and multi-unit buildings are measured against the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for fixture counts, and reduced pressure backflow assemblies are made to ASSE 1013. Ask the property's inspector which edition applies before a larger job starts.

Overnight and holiday calls.

Pipes do not pick a convenient hour. If a supply line gives way at 3 a.m. or on a Sunday, shut the main water valve, move valuables off the floor and call. We offer same-day appointments when available and will tell you on the phone what we can do.

For a leak that has soaked drywall, read leak detection, and for a heater that failed, water heater replacement covers the choices. A drain that keeps backing up is a job for drain cleaning.
