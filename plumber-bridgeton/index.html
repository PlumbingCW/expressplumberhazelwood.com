Local note

Older streets, empty units and winter freezes.

Bridgeton was platted in 1794 and incorporated in 1843, so its streets include very old and very recent housing. Of 5,088 housing units counted in 2020, 9.7 percent were vacant, roughly one in ten. A vacant house with nobody checking it can leak for days, so owners and managers should shut off the main and have someone look in after any hard freeze.

Hazelwood publishes a frost depth of 30 inches and a winter design temperature of 2 degrees F, and Bridgeton is close by. Pipes in unheated garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls take the first hit. Insulating those runs and knowing your shutoff costs far less than a ceiling repair.

Plumbing in Bridgeton is measured against a model code such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) published by IAPMO, depending on what the local authority adopted. Ask the county which edition applies to your address before a larger job begins.

Shutoffs and checks before the plumber arrives.

Do these in order if it is safe, and skip anything that puts you near standing water and outlets.

1Main water valve.Turn it clockwise until it stops. Note whether the water meter keeps spinning.
2Water heater.If water is near the tank, switch off its power or gas first.
3Fixture valves.A sink or toilet leak often has its own small valve under the fixture.
4Standing water.Keep people away from wet outlets and cords.
5Access.Open gates and clear the route to the basement, utility room or cleanout.
