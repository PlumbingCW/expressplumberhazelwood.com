When a backed-up main needs jetting tonight.

A main line that has filled up cannot wait for morning, because the water in it has nowhere to go but back into your house. If a basement floor drain or the lowest tub is bubbling or rising, stop using every fixture. That means no flushing, no washer, no dishwasher and no showers.

Keep people and pets out of any water on the floor, and move rugs and boxes off the slab if you can do it safely. Then call. We schedule the visit any hour, with same-day appointments when available, and we decide on the phone whether a cable can do the job or the line needs the force of a jet.

  • Sewage or gray water rising in a floor drain, tub or shower.
  • Toilets that bubble when the washer drains.
  • A cable cleaning that opened the line, then it clogged again within weeks.
  • A kitchen line that runs slow every time even after snaking.

Jetting or snaking: how the choice gets made.

A cable, also called a snake, bores a hole through a clog. It works well on a sock, a wad of hair or a single root mass. The trouble is that the pipe wall keeps its coating, so the hole closes up again as grease and scale keep building.

A jet uses water at high pressure through a hose with a nozzle that shoots backward and forward. The water cuts the clog loose and scrubs the full circle of the pipe on the way out. Because it reaches the walls, it is the better choice for greasy, scaled or root-packed lines.

What happens during a jetting visit.

We want a look inside before we put a high-pressure hose into any line. A camera pass tells us the pipe material and whether it can take the pressure, and it shows what we are about to clear.

  1. Look before jetting. A quick camera view shows pipe material, sags and breaks, because cracked or collapsed pipe should not be blasted.
  2. Open the access point. The cleanout is the usual entry, and we set the hose so the discharge stays inside the pipe.
  3. Set pressure and nozzle. We pick them for the pipe size, wall condition and what is in the line, not one setting for every house.
  4. Run the line in passes. The nozzle pulls itself downstream, then we draw it back slowly so the water scrubs the pipe wall.
  5. Confirm with water and camera. We flow water through, check nearby fixtures and look again to make sure the line is open.

What hydro jetting cannot fix.

Jetting cleans a pipe. It does not repair one. A line with a crushed section, a belly that holds water or a joint that has slipped apart will clog again, because the problem is in the shape of the pipe and not what is stuck in it.

A badly corroded cast iron pipe can also be too fragile for high pressure, which is another reason we look first. If the camera shows damage, we say so plainly and go over sewer line repair options with you before anything else is done.

Jetting also does not stop roots for good. It clears them back to the pipe wall, and if a joint is open they grow in again. A hard root problem usually ends in a repair or lining, not a yearly jet.

Local note

Hazelwood sewers, roots and who owns the pipe.

Hazelwood is served by the Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District, and MSD maintains the public sewers. The private lateral from your house to the public main is generally the owner's responsibility. Confirm your own case with MSD at 314-768-6260 before you pay for work on the street side of the line.

That matters for jetting, because a clog in the lateral is usually yours to clear, while a problem out in the public main is MSD's. If we find the blockage past your property line, we show you the camera footage so you can take it to MSD. Heavy jetting repeated on a failing lateral is money wasted, so the footage also tells you whether to repair it.

Drain slope is set by the drainage chapters of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and no amount of jetting fixes a line that sags below that slope. When a camera shows a belly, jetting only buys time, and a repair is the lasting answer.

Keeping a jetted line clear.

A line that has just been scoured stays open only as long as the grease and wipes stay out of it. Pour cooking fat into a can, scrape plates into the trash and keep wipes out of the toilet. Those three habits do more for a main line than any additive.

If you want the whole lateral looked at, or the cause of repeat backups found, see sewer camera inspection or drain cleaning for a lighter clog. For homes in the Florissant area, our Florissant plumbing page lists the services we run there.
