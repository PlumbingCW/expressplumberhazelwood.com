What we found about Hazelcrest.

Real estate sites list Hazelcrest Condominiums as a neighborhood in Hazelwood, and individual listings name Hazelcrest Condo and Hazelcrest III Condo. Listings for Hazelcrest Drive and Sieloff Drive give 1965 and 1966 as the year built, and describe brick buildings, one or two stories, with a pool and clubhouse.

Association fees differ by listing. One says the fee includes water, sewer, trash, snow removal and exterior maintenance. Another says it covers all exterior maintenance and leaves the interior to the owner. Both are single listings, so read your own declaration before you assume anything.

When water crosses from one unit to another.

Water that reaches a neighbor involves other people, so the order of steps matters.

  1. Close your own shutoff. Turn off the valve to your unit and stop using water.
  2. Tell management. Report it right away, since a shared line may need to be isolated for the whole building.
  3. Photograph the damage. Take pictures before cleanup, because you may need them for a claim.
  4. Call a plumber for your side. We can find where the water is coming from and fix what belongs to you.
  5. Ask your insurer. Ask whether your condo policy covers the damage and what documents it needs.

If the source is hidden inside a wall, leak detection finds it without opening more drywall than needed. A failed supply line in your unit is a job for burst pipe repair.

Landlords and rental units in Hazelcrest.

The City of Hazelwood requires rental property owners to register each rental unit with the City by January 31 every year. When we checked, the City page listed a $35 fee per property and periodic inspections to check health and safety standards. The page does not say which items inspectors check.

The City also runs a Residential Occupancy Permit Program, and Code Enforcement inspects residential units when occupancy changes. Fixing a leak or a dead water heater quickly, and keeping the invoice, gives you a record if a tenant or inspector asks. See water heater repair for what that work involves.

Utilities and permits for Hazelcrest.

The City of Hazelwood names the Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District for sewer and Missouri American Water for water. In a condo, the association may own the pipes before they reach your unit, so ask management before you call MSD at 314-768-6260 about a line you may not own.

The City says plumbing permits and inspections go through St. Louis County, and Inspections and Permits at 314-513-5034 can explain the process. Some associations also ask for notice or approval before water heater work, so check your declaration first.
