Local note

What Florissant's housing age means for your pipes.

About 18,000 Florissant houses were built between 1947 and 1980, a large share of the city. Plumbing from that era can include cast iron drain stacks, clay or fiber sewer laterals and galvanized supply pipe. That does not mean yours is failing, but it is why we put a camera in a line that clogs again and again before we suggest a repair.

Old Town Florissant is older still. The Old St. Ferdinand Shrine buildings date from 1819 to 1888, and the St. Ferdinand Central Historic District is on the National Register. A house near Old Town may have been added to over time, so we expect mixed pipe materials and check them instead of assuming.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), published by the International Code Council, is the model code Florissant adopted in its 2021 edition. Its drainage and venting chapters are what a Florissant inspector measures a repair against.

Permits and licenses for Florissant work.

Plumbing work in Florissant needs a St. Louis County license, held by a Master Plumber, Journeyman, Registered Plumber or an apprentice working under a Master. Florissant's code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code. Ask any plumber to show you their county license before work starts, ours included.

Owners of detached single-family homes may pull a homeowner permit for their own work, but that excludes gas lines and underground or slab-enclosed piping. When a repair is an emergency, the code allows the permit application within 48 working hours, so a burst pipe at midnight does not wait on paperwork. For a failed supply line, see water line repair and replacement.
